# Bonds and Facets

## Prime 31: structural coupling

Prime 31 gives an organism a generated **Bind** output. When two nearby Prime-31 organisms keep that output active, the simulation can form a bond between them.

Prime 31 is the capacity to form and maintain structure. It is not itself a facet or a predefined body shape.

## Bond (coupling)

A bond is one direct relationship between two organisms. It has:

- strength;
- a small structural-energy reserve;
- reserve maintenance and starvation decay;
- low-energy support transfer between directly connected members;
- one-hop sharing of Prime-13 persistent state.

Each bonded organism also pays the **coupling cost**: currently `0.10` energy per simulation tick. This represents the private energetic cost of remaining coordinated and structurally available. It is separate from the bond reserve's maintenance drain.

An organism that loses Prime 31 through mutation no longer pays this cost, but also loses its ability to form or maintain bonds and to participate in structural ecology.

## Facet

A facet is not explicitly encoded in any organism. It emerges when three bonds form a closed triangle among three organisms:

```text
organism A ─ bond ─ organism B
     ╲                   ╱
      bond           bond
         ╲           ╱
          organism C

= one closed triangular facet
```

So the relationship is:

- Prime 31: capacity to bind.
- Bond/coupling: one direct two-organism relationship.
- Facet: an emergent closed triangle made from three bonds.

## What a strong facet can do

When all three bonds are sufficiently strong, a facet can act as an ecological structure. Current facet-level effects include:

- collective navigation from pooled generated directional scores;
- meal sharing among weaker facet members;
- topology-owned structural capital and inherited bond seeds;
- retention of red catalytic material;
- delayed refinement of detritus or ash;
- maintenance of an engineered fertility gradient after successful restoration;
- bounded local structural nursery opportunities.

These effects belong to the topology, not to an individual organism. If the triangle breaks, the facet no longer exists and cannot maintain its ecological process.

## Why this matters evolutionarily

Prime-31 organisms pay private coupling costs while facets can create public ecological benefits. A mutant that loses Prime 31 may still eat food from an environment maintained by facets without paying structural costs. This public-goods pressure is the main reason structural lineages currently collapse in many experimental conditions.

The research goal is not to make bonds immortal. It is to test whether facets can maintain ecological conditions that make structural persistence naturally selectable.
